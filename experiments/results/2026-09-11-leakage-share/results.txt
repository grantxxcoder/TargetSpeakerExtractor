QUESTION.  D14 gates the whole speaker-state family on one thing: is the other
speaker's voice leaking into our output the dominant cause of transcription
error?  If not, head B has no motivation.

ANSWER.  Yes, it is the largest single cause -- 58.5 % of our wrong content
words are words the interferer actually said -- but it is not all of it, and
41.5 % is words nobody said.

TWO CAUTIONS, both measured here
  1. WeSep has MORE invented words than us in absolute terms (312 vs 308)
     despite solving leakage.  Removing leakage does not remove that residue.
     The addressable prize is ~434 words, not 742.
  2. Our model DELETES more target content than handing over the mixture does
     (11.8 vs 9.3 points; ceiling 0.7).  Head B's pressure is one-sided -- it
     rewards making no non-target voice audible and says nothing about keeping
     the target audible.  Watch deletion rate and enrolment sensitivity on the
     M5 arm, not L_state alone.

VERDICT.  Step 0 passes.  Head B proceeds.
