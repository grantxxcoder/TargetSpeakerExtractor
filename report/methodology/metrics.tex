\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
 The metrics are where the main contribution of the work lies. Since the main task is intelligibility of the target speaker for the listeners and not necessarily the \textit{quality} of the extracted target speaker speech, the metrics that are designed in this section aim to reflect that objective. LCF-WER, ICR and FR measure what the listener recovers and split its errors into leaked and invented words, while DNSMOS and the signal-to-distortion ratios are the conventional measures compared against them.

\subsubsection{Transcripts and references}
\label{sec:transcripts}
When a system produces output, either the offline or the live model with the prompt of Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}, it gives a transcript. The two anchors get transcribed as well to give the untouched mixture and the clean target. Every transcript is scored against the LibriSpeech reference transcript of the target speaker, and the interferer speaker. Before scoring, each transcript is normalised using Whisper's \texttt{EnglishTextNormalizer} \cite{whisper_normalizer,radford2023whisper}. This includes lowercasing, removing punctuation, expanding contractions and writing numbers consistently.

\subsubsection{Listener Content Fidelity Word Error Rate}
\label{sec:lcfwer}
The Listener Content Fidelity Word Error Rate (LCF-WER) is a word error rate applied to a live-model transcription of the audio. The equation is as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \textup{WER} = \frac{S + D + I}{N}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the number of substitutions, $D$ is the number of deletions, $I$ is the number of insertions, and $N$ is the total number of words in the reference. It is essentially a minimum edit-distance formula, where the number of edits required to change a reference text into the proposed text is divided by the number of words in the reference text. The metric can go above 100\,\%.


\subsubsection{Interferer Content Rate}
\label{sec:icr}
The Interferer Content Rate (ICR) is the first metric used to highlight one particular nuance of the LCF-WER metric: when the live model transcribes a word that the target never said, that word was either invented by the model or heard from the interfering speaker. ICR measures the second case (\ref{ai:metrics}).

\paragraph{Content words.} The ICR counts only content words. Function words such as \emph{the}, \emph{and} and \emph{of} are removed with the NLTK English stopword list \cite{bird2009nltk}, because almost any two English sentences share them. An overlap on these words would say nothing about whether the other speaker's content reached the listener. These content words will be used to make an interferer-exclusive bag of words, which indicates which words the model picks up from the interfering speaker. Specifically, a content word is \emph{available to leak} on trial $i$ if the interfering speaker said it and the target did not. Each word is counted once per trial: $a_i$ is the number of words available to leak on trial $i$, and $\ell_i$ the number of those that leaked.

\paragraph{The rate.} The number of leaked words grows with how much the interfering speaker said, so the count is turned into two rates. The first asks how often a trial leaks. For a set of trials $\mathcal{T}$ and a threshold of $k$ words,
\begin{equation}
\textup{ICR}@k = \frac{\big|\{\, i \in \mathcal{T}_k \;:\; \ell_i \ge k \,\}\big|}{|\mathcal{T}_k|},
\qquad\mathcal{T}_k = \{\, i \in \mathcal{T} \;:\; a_i \ge k \,\},
\label{eq:icrk}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}$ is the set of trials being scored, $i$ indexes a trial in it, $\ell_i$ is the number of leaked words on trial $i$, $a_i$ is the number of words available to leak on trial $i$, $k$ is the number of leaked words at which a trial counts as leaking, and $\mathcal{T}_k$ is the subset of trials with at least $k$ words available to leak.
Only trials with at least $k$ available words are eligible, since a trial with a single available word can never reach $\textup{ICR}@2$ and would only dilute the rate. This work uses $k = 2$: one shared content word can be coincidence, while two is a signal. The choice does not change any conclusion, as every $k$ from 1 to 5 fully separates the no-processing and target-alone reference points (\autoref{sec:anchors}). The second rate asks how much leaks, as the share of the available words that reached the transcript, expressed as a percentage:
\begin{equation}
\textup{mean leak} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}} \frac{\ell_i}{a_i}.
\label{eq:meanleak}
\end{equation}                                                                                                                              
Lower percentages are better for both metrics.                               
                                                                                                                                            
\subsubsection{Fabrication Rate}
\label{sec:fr}
The Fabrication Rate (FR) highlights the case where the model has invented a word that is present in neither the target nor the interferer and is only computable with exact transcriptions as given in LibriSpeech~\cite{panayotov2015librispeech}. The FR metric is defined as follows:
Over the set $\mathcal{T}$ of trials with a non-empty response, the statistic is the mean invented count per trial of the number of words in the model output that are not present in either the target or interferer, 
\begin{equation}
    \textup{FR}_{\textup{count}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}} \big| \mathcal{I}_i \big|,
    \label{eq:frcount}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_i$ is the set of words in the $i$-th trial's output that are not present in either the target or interferer (\ref{ai:metrics}). A rate of fabrication is also defined by whether a trial contains at least $k$ invented words as defined in \eqref{eq:frk}.  
\begin{equation}
    \textup{FR}@k = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}} \mathbf{1}\! \left[\, \big| \mathcal{I}_i \big| \ge k \,\right],
    \qquad k = 2 \text{ chosen in this work,}
    \label{eq:frk}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{1}\!$ is the indicator function. An invented word is not necessarily a hallucination as defined by \cite{koenecke2024careless}: a misheard word is counted as invented, but it is not a hallucination. 

\subsubsection{Deep Noise Suppression Mean Opinion Score}
\label{sec:dnsmos}
DNSMOS \cite{reddy2021dnsmos,reddy2022dnsmos835} is a neural network that predicts what a collection of humans would have rated an audio clip on a scale of 1--5, where 1 is bad and 5 is excellent. Two variants are reported: ITU-T P.835 \cite{itu2003p835}, which gives speech quality (SIG), background quality (BAK) and overall quality (OVRL), and ITU-T P.808 \cite{itu2018p808}, a single score that the REAL-TSE organisers adopted after entrants were found to game the P.835 metric. 

\subsubsection{Signal-to-Distortion/Interference/Artefact Ratios}
\label{sec:sdiar}
SDR is used to measure how wrong the output audio is in total, and the SIR and SAR are used to measure in which direction the output audio is wrong. The SIR measures how much real audio that is not target speech is present in the output audio such as an interfering speaker or background noise, and the SAR measures how much of the output audio is not real audio, such as artefacts from the model. The output is split by orthogonal projection \cite{vincent2006bsseval} into the target part $s_\textup{proj}$ of \eqref{eq:alpha}, the interference $e_\textup{int}$ explained by the other speaker $s_\textup{int}$ and the noise, and the artefact $e_\textup{art}$ explained by none of the sources, so that $\hat{s} = s_\textup{proj} + e_\textup{int} + e_\textup{art}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\textup{SDR} &= 10\log_{10} \dfrac{\lVert s_\textup{proj}\rVert^2}{\lVert e_\textup{int} + e_\textup{art}\rVert^2 + \varepsilon_\textup{pres} \lVert s_\textup{proj}\rVert^2}, \\
\textup{SIR} &= 10\log_{10} \dfrac{\lVert s_\textup{proj}\rVert^2}{\lVert e_\textup{int}\rVert^2 + \varepsilon_\textup{pres} \lVert s_\textup{proj}\rVert^2}, \\
\textup{SAR} &= 10\log_{10} \dfrac{\lVert s_\textup{proj} + e_\textup{int}\rVert^2}{\lVert e_\textup{art}\rVert^2 + \varepsilon_\textup{pres} \lVert s_\textup{proj} + e_\textup{int}\rVert^2},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sdr-sir-sar}
\end{equation}
which are all measured in decibels (dB) where the higher ratio means better extraction of the target from the other audio in question. Each denominator is floored at $\varepsilon_\textup{pres} = 10^{-3}$ of its numerator, as in \eqref{eq:lpres}, which caps every ratio at \SI[retain-explicit-plus]{+30}{\decibel}. 

\subsubsection{Reference points}                                  
\label{sec:anchors}
The following terms are used to describe the lower and upper bounds of the results section. \emph{No processing} refers to passing the full mixture audio, unprocessed, to a listener as the worst-case scenario, and \emph{target alone} refers to passing the clean target audio to the listener as the best case. Target alone is perfect separation, where the listener hears only the target audio. Neither is a real system; both are reference points to compare the extractors against. 
\subsubsection{Uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty}
Each 95\,\% interval is a percentile bootstrap \cite{efron1979bootstrap}. The interval is computed using bootstrap resampling where the trials are redrawn with replacement 10{,}000 times, and on every draw the score is recomputed as the total errors over the total reference words \cite{bisani2004bootstrap}. Because trials from the same speaker are not independent, unique speakers are chosen rather than individual trials, and whole meetings for the AMI recordings.
