\section{Speech LLMs as listeners and judges}
\label{sec:llms}

  - Target speaker extraction: Conditioning on a voice sample makes extraction possible for unseen speakers, but systems can still extract the 
    wrong speaker and are rarely trained to fall silent when the target is inactive \cite{zmolikova2023overview}. This work conditions on both a 
    spectral cue and a speaker embedding, trains on target-absent trials, and measures wrong-speaker output separately (Sections X, Y).
  - Streaming separation: Online systems are verified against a latency limit \cite{wang2026realtse}, but their output is judged on signal and 
    perceptual metrics and a single offline recogniser, not on what a downstream live model understands. This work builds a causal extractor 
    within a 200–300 ms budget and judges it by the live model (Section Z).
  - How extraction is evaluated: Signal metrics do not track recognition, perceptual metrics have been gamed \cite{wang2026realtse}, and no 
    standard metric separates a damaged target from a wrongly chosen one \cite{zmolikova2023overview}. This work defines three content scores that
    do (Section M), and reports the conventional metrics beside them to show where they disagree.
  - Speech LLMs as listeners and judges: Language models have been studied as judges of quality, but not as the listener that decides whether a 
    front-end preserved what was said. This work uses a live speech model as that listener and measures how much its answers vary between runs 
    (Section J). Every result is therefore specific to Gemini.
