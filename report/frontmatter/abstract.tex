\section*{Abstract}
This work builds a causal, real-time, single-channel target speaker extractor whose output is judged by a live speech-to-speech model, Gemini. This work analyses how to build an extractor whose output improves intelligibility for a live model, rather than optimising for signal separation quality. Two errors are considered: words leaked from the interfering speaker and words that neither speaker said that the live model invented or mis-transcribed. An evaluation suite is thus proposed to consider both errors and states the relationship between them. The extractor built is a causal band-split network, conditioned on an enrolment clip of the target speaker. The extractor is trained on a simulated dataset created from LibriSpeech speakers, with random room acoustics and background noise. Since the live model cannot be differentiated through, a proxy loss is used to train the extractor. Splitting the cue and adding a speaker embedding to the extractor showed improvement over the standard band separation architecture, where the Gemini word error rate dropped from 53.26\,\% for the baseline to 39.59\,\% for the extension, while nearly halving the leakage of the interfering speaker's words. The model is seen to perform in real time with a \SI{175}{\milli\second} latency on a laptop CPU. The extractor is evaluated on real recordings, holding the same conclusions as the simulated data. The signal metrics align with the ranking set out by Gemini, but do not predict whether more of the target is recovered on a given trial. Perceptual metrics do not follow the ranking set out by Gemini. The extension beats just passing the mixture straight to the live model by dropping the word error rate from 62.94\,\% to 39.59\,\%, the rate at which two or more words are leaked in a trial drops from 75.73\,\% to 33.66\,\%, the share of the interfering speaker's words reaching Gemini drops from 63.90\,\% to 23.08\,\%, but at the cost of an increased rate of two or more words fabricated from 30.74\,\% to 46.88\,\%. Finally, the extension is compared against an established offline system, WeSep, which is trained on different data and is not optimised for the live model. WeSep recovers more of the target when both speakers talk, however the different data strategy used in this work shows better performance when no target is present. All results are for at most two-speaker mixtures and are optimised for Gemini.
