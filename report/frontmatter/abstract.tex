\section*{Abstract}
This work builds a causal, real-time, single-channel target speaker extractor whose output is judged by a live speech-to-speech model, Gemini. This work analyses how to build an extractor whose output improves intelligibility for a live model, rather than optimising for signal separation quality. Two errors are considered: words leaked from the interfering speaker and words that neither speaker said that the live model invented or mis-transcribed. An evaluation suite is thus proposed to consider both errors and states the relationship between them. The extractor built is a causal band-split network, conditioned on an enrollment clip of the target speaker. The extractor is trained on a simulated dataset created from LibriSpeech speakers, with random room acoustics and background noise. Since the live model cannot be differentiated through, a proxy loss is used to train the extractor. Splitting the cue and adding a speaker embedding to the extractor showed improvement over the standard band separation architecture, where the Gemini word error rate on the test set dropped from 48.46\,\% for the baseline to 41.81\,\% for the extension, while cutting the leakage of the interfering speaker's words by about a third. The model is seen to perform in real time with a \SI{175}{\milli\second} latency on a laptop CPU. The extractor is evaluated on real recordings, holding the same conclusions as the simulated data. Perceptual and signal-domain metrics do not convincingly follow the ranking set out by Gemini. On the test set, the extension beats just passing the mixture straight to the live model by dropping the word error rate from 52.05\,\% to 41.81\,\%, the rate at which two or more words are leaked in a trial drops from 60.57\,\% to 32.93\,\%, the share of the interfering speaker's words reaching Gemini drops from 47.89\,\% to 21.15\,\%, but at the cost of an increased rate of two or more invented words from 30.24\,\% to 47.74\,\%. Finally, the extension is compared against an established offline system, WeSep, which is trained on different data and is not optimised for the live model. WeSep recovers more of the target whenever the target speaks, however the extension is able to remove more of the interfering speaker's energy (SIR: extension 10.09, WeSep 8.40\,dB), keep the other speaker out when the target is absent (words per trial: extension 12.88, WeSep 17.35) and run in real time at half of WeSep's delay. All results are for at most two-speaker mixtures and are optimised for Gemini.
