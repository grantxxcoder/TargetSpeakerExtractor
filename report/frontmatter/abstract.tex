\section*{Abstract}
This work builds a causal, real-time, single-channel target speaker extractor whose output is judged by a live speech-to-speech model, Gemini. This work analyses how to build an extractor whose output improves intelligibility for a live model, rather than optimising for signal separation quality. Two errors are considered: words leaked from the interfering speaker and words that neither speaker said that the live model invented or mis-transcribed. An evaluation suite is thus proposed to consider both errors and states the trade-off between them. The extractor built is a causal band-split network, conditioned on an enrollment clip of the target speaker. The extractor is trained on a simulated dataset created from LibriSpeech speakers, with random room acoustics and background noise. Since the live model cannot be differentiated through, a proxy loss is used to train the extractor. Splitting the cue and adding a speaker embedding to the extractor showed improvement over the standard band separation architecture, where the Gemini word error rate dropped from 53.3\,\% for the baseline to 39.6\,\% for the extension, while nearly halving the leakage of the interfering speaker's words. The model is seen to perform in real time with a \SI{175}{\milli\second} latency on a laptop CPU. The extractor is evaluated on real recordings, holding the same conclusions as the simulated data. Perceptual metrics show how the extractor worsens the perception of the audio relative to the original mixture, but did not indicate the live model's perception of the audio. The extension beats just passing the mixture straight to the live model by dropping the word error rate from 62.94\,\% to 39.6\,\%, the rate at which two or more words are leaked in a trial drops from 75.73\,\% to 33.66\,\%, the mean number of words leaked from the interfering speaker drops from 63.90\,\% to 23.08\,\%, but at the cost of increased rate of two or or more words fabricated from 30.7\,\% to 46.9\,\%. Finally, the extension yields a more stable output than any tested system including an established offline system, WeSep, which is trained on different data and is not optimised for the live model. However, WeSep recovers more of the target when both speakers talk. All results are for at most two-speaker mixtures and are optimised for Gemini.
