\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The work in this report set out to build a target speaker extractor that could prepare audio for a live speech-to-speech model under the constraints of being able to run in real time, causally and on a single channel of audio. The extractor was judged by what Gemini recovers from the extracted output, and not by the actual separation quality of target speech from the other audio. All of the findings relate to at most two-speaker mixtures, and optimised and tested for Gemini.

The simple word error rate hides the trade-off that matters for the downstream model. The word error rate alone does not discern how insertions or substitutions are obtained, and both are rooted in different causes. The causes include either invention or leakage, and an extractor needs to tradeoff optimising one against the other. The next finding includes the confirmation for how signal, perceptual and offline measures do not correlate with the live model's perception of the audio. Despite being needed to train an extractor by forming loss terms from the metrics, the metrics can only act as a proxy for how the model extracts and not necessarily how well the extractor performs for the downstream model. The next finding is that telling the extractor more about the target's voice is what helped the most. This involved considering the enrollment and the conditioning of the extractor on the target speaker's embedding. The cue was split into parts and adding the speaker embedding lowered Gemini's word error rate from 53.3\,\% for the baseline to 39.6\,\%. The final finding is that the live model does not have much tolerance for artefacts where even with the extension Gemini invented 2.20 words per trial where doing nothing only 1.27.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The structure and nature of the approach formulated in this work allows for a number of future directions to be explored. The first suggestion is considering the streaming architecture itself. The LSTM layers of the extractor could be replaced with state space layers. A state space layer is a recurrent layer whose updates are linear, which allows for parallel training while still running frame by frame. SA-Mamba \cite{liu2026samamba} applies this idea within a band-split, dual-path design similar to the one in this work, and conditions the state space layers on the target speaker. It placed second in the online track of the REAL-TSE challenge, while its report describes training on simulated mixtures only, and the organisers attribute most of the gains of the top systems to their data \cite{wang2026realtse}. The architecture has not yet been used with a live speech model as the listener and secondly it did not have a training recipe built around one, as in this work. The next suggestion would be to consider the current objective function. It is a proxy for general extraction rather than for the downstream task. Weighting the leaked speech or the artefacts in the loss moved errors from one source to the other but did not lower the LCF-WER (\autoref{sec:results_metric}), so an objective closer to \emph{how} Gemini listens is still needed. Another idea would be to address how the extractor struggles with the trade-off between removing interfering speech and introducing artefacts. A likely reason is the aggressive masking, which can leave sharp transitions where the interfering speech was removed. The extractor could instead be trained to also extract the interfering speaker, so that it learns what to remove as well as what to keep. Rather than leaving gaps where the interfering speech was removed, the extractor could fill them with a signal that Gemini ignores, such as low-level noise, leaving nothing for Gemini to fill with words. Gemini, during experimentation, was found to be robust to very low-level noise, but the idea is to make audio clips that are optimised to be ignored by the listener. Finally, I believe there is potential in the presence gate, which was only trained on the extension. I would suggest that the gate rather be trained on the two speaker condition rather than a general target present condition. Right now the gate only theoretically helps for interferer only trials, but this should be modified to help prevent leakage in the two-speaker condition. This could be in the form of allowing the gate to close on the interferer's frames when the target is present.
