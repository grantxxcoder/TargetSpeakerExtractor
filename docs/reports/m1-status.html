Where it stands

The baseline is assembled component by component, and each component was measured before it was accepted.

The week moved the project from data to model. The streaming BSRNN baseline now exists as working components — STFT and inverse STFT, the band-split planner and slicer, per-band normalisation, the residual LSTM block, the estimation module, and the TF-Map enrollment conditioning that turns speech enhancement into target speaker extraction. The architecture is settled at 7.16 M parameters, chosen to sit deliberately below the challenge baselines rather than accidentally.

The part worth reporting is not that the components exist but how the choices were settled. Three of them were decided by measuring rather than by following the source paper, and one source choice was rejected because measurement contradicted it.

ChoiceSettled asBecause
Normalisation Channel-wise LayerNorm, per band, per frame BatchNorm optimises a different function than it deploys, and the gap is wide here. GroupNorm was measured to leak future information.
Enrollment conditioning TF-Map spectral similarity, not embedding similarity The embedding path needs a speaker encoder over the live stream — unaffordable at this latency, and it adds parameters to a model kept small on purpose.
Silence weight w = 0.458 Derived from this project's measured 0.297 crop-level absent rate, not copied from the source corpus's rate.
Spectral term weight wm = 9.62 Set by measuring both terms on a do-nothing output over 300 crops, not by sweeping.
DNSMOS loss term Rejected The challenge organisers measured it at LCC +0.003 against human opinion on the online track. Optimising it is the exact failure this project's metric exists to avoid.

The training objective is now fully specified: a target-present term, a multi-resolution spectral term to cover what that term is blind to, and a target-absent term for the 29.7 % of crops containing no target speech. It departs from the closest published objective in six places, all logged with reasons — most consequentially a correction to a scale-invariance error in the published form, where an output of the correct shape scores better simply for being made louder.

Finding to flag

The conditioning module does something slightly different from what its own paper describes.

TF-Map is presented as matching each incoming frame against the enrollment frames that resemble it. It cannot do that as published. Magnitude spectra are non-negative, so the cosine similarities entering its softmax lie in [0, 1], which bounds the ratio between the largest and smallest weight at e — and spread over a five-second enrollment that is nearly uniform. Measured on a training trial: largest weight 0.00239 against a uniform 0.00159, with essentially every enrollment frame contributing.

What this means

What the module actually supplies is the target's average spectral shape, scaled each frame by how much of the mixture lies along it — not the frame-selective matching the description implies. It is still a useful cue, and it holds for the published form too. Sharpening its selectivity is now a Chapter 4 experiment rather than an assumption.
